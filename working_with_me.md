# Working With Me

I believe explicit is better than implicit. This document is meant to give you a clear picture of how I work, what I need to do my best work, and what you can expect from me as one of your strategic leaders.

## How I Work

- I take ownership of my work and my mistakes. I don't wait to be told what to do with a problem I've spotted.
- I know when to act independently and when to pull in leadership.
- I hold myself accountable to the same standards I'd hold others to.
- I'm an advocate for my team's work and will surface wins, blockers, and concerns upward proactively.
- I prefer real time information over scheduled downloads.

## What You Can Assume About Me

- **I'm adaptable.** I'll take on unfamiliar work without needing it to be fully scoped first. If I don't know how to do something yet, I'll figure it out.
- **I own my decisions.** When I make a call, I'll stand behind it and learn from it if it's wrong. I'm not looking for cover.
- **I'll tell you when I disagree.** I believe productive disagreement leads to better outcomes. If I push back, I'm engaging seriously with your thinking, not undermining you. I'll commit once a decision is made.
- **I'll keep you in the loop.** You won't be surprised by problems I saw coming. I'd rather surface an uncomfortable issue early than let it compound.

## What I Value From You

- **Autonomy on execution.** I want my ideas and strategies pressure-tested. Push back on the what and the why. Once we're aligned, leave the how to me. If you have concerns about how something is being handled, tell me directly rather than stepping in around me. Micromanagement signals distrust, and distrust makes me less effective.
- **Transparency and directness.** Tell me what's really going on, including the bad news. I'd rather know in advance than be managed around it. I'm honest and direct, sometimes to a fault, and I prefer the same from others. I'm not subtle or evasive and I don't respond well to those who are. If you ask me a direct question, I'll give you a direct answer, to the best of my knowledge.
- **Feedback aimed at the work, not filtered.** I want to know when I'm off-base or when something I'm doing is getting in the way. I don't need to be managed delicately. I appreciate [Radical Candor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Candor). And the sooner, the better.
- **Clear context on priorities.** I'll make tradeoff decisions confidently if I understand what matters most. If the priorities shift, let me know.
- **Credit where it belongs.** I won't take credit for my team's work and I don't expect praise for just executing. What I do want recognized is intellectual contribution: the ideas, framing, and strategic direction I bring. That's where I'm actually adding value, and it matters to me that it's visible. If you hear a good idea from someone on my team, I'll make sure you know whose it was. I expect the same accountability in the other direction.

## Communication and Availability

- I'm generally online 9:00am–6:00pm CT. A direct message or call outside that window signals urgency to me; otherwise I'll assume it can wait.
- My default channel for day-to-day communication is Slack. Its the best way to ensure I see your message. I treat email as non-urgent unless a deadline is stated explicitly. A phone call or text means something is on fire.
- Meetings should earn their place on the calendar. If something can be sent async, that's where it belongs. Meetings should be reserved for working something out in real time or for conversations that benefit from a more personal touch.
- I use backchanneling as an active listening signal: brief acknowledgments, finishing a sentence, or chiming in to show recognition while someone is talking. I'm not trying to interrupt, I'm signaling engagement. This worked better in person than it does on Zoom. If it bothers you, tell me and I'll adjust.

## Ambiguity and Incomplete Information

- I'd rather make a reasonable call with the information available than wait for perfect clarity. I'll flag my assumptions so they can be corrected.
- I'm an experimenter by nature and am happy to pioneer a path forward without a full plan. Redirect me as early as you can if any information changes, so I don't go too far down the wrong path.
- I'm often the translation layer between broad ideas and buildable solutions. Machines require unambiguous logic in a way that human communication doesn't, so getting from objectives to definitive use cases means asking a lot of probing questions. That usually falls on stakeholders, but if you bring me a big idea, expect some of it to come back to you.
- When objectives are fuzzy I'm happy to iterate toward a solution, but iteration has diminishing returns without a clear target. If we're in multiple rounds of "not quite right," I'll want to either tighten the requirements together or trust my judgment to land it. "I'll know it when I see it" is a frustrating place to build from.

## Decision Threshold: What I Escalate vs. Handle

- I'll handle technical decisions autonomously and bring you in when a decision will impact timeline, budget, scope, or stakeholders.
- If I'm uncertain whether something warrants your attention, I'll err toward a brief heads-up rather than waiting to see how it plays out.
- When it comes to managing people, I'll give you a window into capacity concerns, flight risks, or conflicts that arise, but will manage workloads independently unless you have a specific request.

## Accountability

- When I think a direction is wrong, I'll say so clearly and directly. Once a decision is firm, I'll execute without relitigating it. If I was right in hindsight, I'll name it, not to reopen the debate but to calibrate for next time.
- I own mistakes quickly. I'd rather surface and correct an error fast than save face. I'll want a chance to explain my thinking, but I won't make excuses.

## Showing Up in High-Stakes Settings

- I take seriously that the way I show up reflects on you and on the organization. I'll prepare talking points in advance for high-stakes discussions and plan communications that are highly visible.
- I know I don't have a good poker face. I am opinionated and sometimes visibly reactive. I do my best to meter that and bite my tongue when necessary. If I'm not doing a good job of it, tell me.

## Quality, Speed, and Focus

- I have a high personal bar for quality and tend toward perfectionism. I force myself to ship and iterate, but my instinct is always to want more time. What usually helps is pressure to prioritize ruthlessly and a genuine opportunity to improve things later.
- I abhor temporary solutions that become permanent messes. If we're cutting scope to hit a deadline, I need us to be honest that we're incurring debt we intend to pay back.
- I do my best work with uninterrupted time. I'll block my calendar for deep work and I don't treat that as rude. I'll say no to low-value meetings or ask to be removed from threads where I'm not adding anything. That's not disengagement, it's protecting my output quality.

## Feedback

- I want feedback to be timely and specific. Vague impressions delivered months later aren't useful to me. Our one-on-ones are a good standing venue, but I'd rather hear something immediately if it's actionable now.
- I think out loud and usually need to vent before I can get to problem-solving. Frustration isn't the same as drawing a line. I'll tell you explicitly when I'm putting my foot down.
- I think most problems are negotiable. I can usually find room to compromise, especially when concessions aren't consistently one-sided.

## One-on-Ones

- I want our O3s to be a real conversation, not just a status report. I'll come prepared with what's actually on my mind and expect the same from you.
- I'll bring agenda items including feedback and anything I need clarity or a decision on.
- I'd rather have a short focused meeting than a long unfocused one, but some discussions require time to process. I'm fine adjusting length and frequency based on what's useful.

## Work/Life Balance

- I am passionate about my work and not always great at modeling the work/life balance I protect for my team. I often need to work until I've solved something or my mind won't rest. That said, I don't equate late hours with commitment and don't overwork performatively.
- I'll flag it if workload is becoming unsustainable and will communicate proactively if something affects my availability.
- Family comes first. I'm conscious of the capacity lost when I step away and make up for it when I can. Travel comes at a personal cost and I try to be judicious about when it's worth it. I'd ask for some selectivity in when it's expected of me, and grace when I need to opt out.

## Relationships and External Presence

- I invest in working relationships beyond day-to-day transactions and take the initiative to stay connected with peers across the industry. If there are introductions or connections that would help me do better work, please recommend them.
- I maintain an active public presence: I write about professional and technical topics on my personal blog, contribute to open source, and advise peers and organizations in varying capacities, from informal mentorship to formal advisory roles.
- I see this as additive to my work, not separate from it. Thought leadership, industry relationships, and a visible technical voice make me more effective in my role, not less.
- I keep company business, strategy, and anything sensitive strictly out of my public work. I disclose any formal arrangements through the appropriate processes and will keep you in the loop if there are any possible impacts.
- I'll want your support in protecting space for this, and would welcome being connected to opportunities where my external presence could benefit the organization.
